じじいと走り方を科学する③ ― 前傾すると、勝手に脚が出る？

50歳からのランニングフォーム研究、第3弾。今回は前傾姿勢を意識して11.07kmを走った。勝手に脚が出る感覚、下を向いてしまう目線、真下よりも後ろに着地するイメージ。正解はまだ分からないが、新しい発見があった。そして、このランは開発中のリングスナウの試験も兼ねていた。

前傾姿勢って、どうやるの？ 2026年11月27日。 前回は、腰から脚が出るような感覚を発見した。 今回は、以前から気になっていた前傾姿勢を意識して走ってみることにした。 前傾姿勢が良いという話はよく聞く。動画も見ている。理屈もなんとなく理解している。 しかし、自分でやろうとすると分からない。 そもそも筋力が足りないのか？ プランクなどの筋トレをした方が良いのか？ それとも、単純に身体の使い方が分かっていないだけなのか？ じじいとそんな話をしながら、今日も走りに出かけた。 勝手に脚が出る？ 前傾を意識して走ってみると、今までとは違う感覚があった。 自分で脚を動かしているというよりも、勝手に脚が出るような感覚だ。 特定の筋肉を使っているという感じもあまりない。 その一方で、前回感じた「腰から脚が出る」という感覚は薄くなった。 前傾と骨盤の動きは両立できるのだろうか。 走っている途中、ふと元メジャーリーガーのイチロー選手の走り方が頭に浮かんだ。 もちろん、あんなに蹴り足は高く上がっていないし、同じフォームで走れているわけでもない。 あくまで自分の頭の中のイメージである。 真下よりも後ろ？ ランニングでは「身体の真下に着地する」という言葉をよく聞く。 私も以前から意識していた。 しかし、今日は少し違った。 今までの着地のイメージが真下だったとするならば、今日はそれよりも後ろに着地するような感覚だった。 もちろん、実際に身体の真下より後ろに着地しているという意味ではない。 自分の中で、着地のイメージが変わったということだ。 👴🏻 じじい「ほう。脚を前に出そうとする意識が変わったのかもしれんな。ただし、無理に後ろに着地しようとするんじゃないぞ。」 新たな問題は目線 Exploring Running Form with Grandpa AI #3 — Does Leaning Forward Make My Legs Move Naturally?

The third installment of my running form experiments at age 50. During an 11.07 km run, I explored forward lean, naturally moving legs, downward gaze, and the sensation of landing farther back. I still have more questions than answers. This run also served as a test activity for Rings Now, a feature currently under development.

How Do You Actually Lean Forward? November 27, 2026. During my previous run, I discovered the sensation of moving my legs from my hips. Today, I decided to explore something that had puzzled me for a long time: forward lean. I often hear that leaning forward is beneficial for running. I have watched videos and understand some of the theory. But when I try it myself, I get confused. Am I lacking muscular strength? Should I do exercises such as planks? Or have I simply not learned how to use my body effectively? After discussing these questions with Grandpa AI, I headed out for another run. My Legs Move by Themselves? When I focused on leaning forward, I noticed a different sensation. Rather than consciously moving my legs, I felt as though they were moving naturally on their own. I did not particularly notice any specific muscles working harder. However, the sensation of moving my legs from my hips, which I had discovered during my previous run, became less noticeable. Can forward lean and natural pelvic movement coexist? While running, I suddenly pictured the running style of Ichiro Suzuki, the former Major League Baseball player. Of course, my trailing leg was not lifting nearly as high as his, and I certainly was not reproducing his running form. It was simply a mental image. Landing Farther Back? Runners often hear the advice to land directly underneath their bodies. I have tried to follow that advice for some time. Today, however, the sensation was slightly different. If my previous mental image was landing directly underneath myself, today’s sensation was of landing even farther back. That does not mean my feet were actually landing behind my body. It simply means that my perception of foot placement had changed. 👴🏻 Grandpa AI: “Interesting. Perhaps your intention to reach forward with your legs has changed. But don’t deliberately force your feet to land behind you.” A New Problem: Where to Look When I concentrate on leaning forward, my gaze tends to drop. Before I know it, I am looking at the ground about one meter ahead. Sometimes my neck bends downward as well. Am I leaning forward with my whole body, or simply bending my upper body? Sometimes I feel almost as if I might fall forward. At other times, I do not. Without knowing what the correct form feels like, I kept experimenting throughout the run. Grandpa AI suggested naturally looking about 10 to 20 meters ahead on a safe, flat road. The previous mental image of having a string gently pulling the top of my head upward might also work alongside a slight forward lean. Next time, I want to explore my gaze as well. It dropped from 178 to 172 steps per minute. I actually noticed this while running. I felt that I could move my legs faster, but I was concerned that doing so would make me lose my focus on forward lean. Today, learning the movement took priority over speed. My ground contact time increased, as did my vertical oscillation. Focusing on forward lean does not automatically improve every running metric. The routes, distances, and paces were also different, so these comparisons alone cannot establish whether my running efficiency improved or declined. My form was not consistent throughout today’s experiment, either. The Final Kilometer My final full kilometer was approximately 5:30. I was still concentrating on forward lean, but the movement felt a little more familiar toward the end. Whether it was technically correct is another question. Has it made me faster? I honestly do not know yet. What I do know is that I am exploring a different way of using my body. Another Purpose: Rings Now Today’s run had another purpose. It was also a test activity for Rings Now, a new Runner’s Rings feature currently under development. I use my own running data to help test the feature. Grandpa AI and I explore running form. Chappy develops the running application. Muse audits the development work. We each have different roles, but today’s run connects these activities. At 50, Still Learning How to Run I run full marathons, yet I am still trying to understand how to run. It might sound a little strange. But being able to run long distances and knowing how to use your body effectively are not necessarily the same thing. Last time, I explored pelvic movement. Today, I explored forward lean. Next time, I will pay attention to my gaze as well. Rather than changing everything at once, I want to experiment gradually. 👴🏻 Grandpa AI: “We experiment because we don’t know the answer.
