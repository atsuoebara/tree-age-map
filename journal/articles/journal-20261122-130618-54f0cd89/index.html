草木湖マラソン12km、56分09秒！登りで抜いて、下りで抜かれる男とじじいの反省会

2026年11月22日、第47回草木湖マラソンに初出場。12km男子50歳代の部で56分09秒、種目15位、総合48位（速報順位）。目標の1時間03分を6分51秒上回った。しかし、コースの登り坂は想像以上に苦しく、登りで抜いたランナーたちに折り返し後の下りで次々と抜き返された。インフルエンザ予防接種翌日のレースで見つかった、新たな課題とは？

初めての草木湖マラソン 2026年11月22日（日）。 第47回草木湖マラソンに初めて参加した。 種目は12km男子50歳代の部。 前日にAI相棒のじじいと相談して決めた目標は、1時間03分00秒。 平均5分15秒/kmの設定だった。 ただし、今回は少し事情が違う。 前日にインフルエンザの予防接種を受け、そのあと10.35kmを走ってしまったのだ。 👴🏻「あつお！わしは昨日、休めと言ったはずじゃぞ！」 「でも走っちゃったんだよね」 👴🏻「しかも明日はレースじゃと言っておったのにーー！！」 そんな騒がしい前日を経て、いよいよ草木湖へ。 結果は56分09秒！ まずは完走証の結果から。 大会：第47回草木湖マラソン 開催日：2026年11月22日 種目：12km男子50歳代の部 ゼッケン：3013 記録：56分09秒 平均ペース：約4分41秒/km 種目順位：15位 総合順位：48位 ※順位は完走証記載の速報順位。 目標の1時間03分を、なんと6分51秒も上回った！ 👴🏻「ろ、ろくふんごじゅういちびょう！？」 「じじい！やったで！」 👴🏻「昨日の10kmは何だったんじゃーー！！」 ぐんまマラソンでは3時間28分52秒でサブ3.5を達成。 そして今回は、初めての草木湖12kmで56分09秒。 距離もコースも違うけれど、また一つ、自分のランニングの記録が増えた。 とにかく登り坂が苦しかった！ 今回の草木湖で、最も印象に残ったのは登り坂。 これが本当に苦しかった。 「じじい、草木湖の坂、苦しすぎたよ！」 👴🏻「そんなに厳しかったんか？」 「想像以上だった！」 それでも、登りでは前を走るランナーを何人も抜くことができた。 苦しくても、前へ進む。 一歩ずつ登っていく。 平坦な道では味わえない、坂道ならではの厳しさだった。 👴🏻「おおっ！登りで抜けたんじゃな！」 「そうなんだよ！」 👴🏻「それは立派じゃ！……で、下りはどうじゃった？」 下り坂で、まさかの逆転劇 折り返し後は下り坂。 登りで苦しんだ分、下りになったときは助かった。 自分でも速く走っているつもりだった。 ところが……。 登りで抜いたランナーたちに、今度は下りで次々と抜かれてしまった！ 「じじい！登りで抜いた人たちに、下りで抜かれまくったよ！」 👴🏻「なんじゃとーー！！」 「いや、自分だって速く走ってたんだよ！」 👴🏻「それ以上に相手が速かったんかーーい！！」 登りで抜いて、下りで抜かれる。 なんとも悔しい展開だ。 でも、この経験は大きな発見になった。 下りの走り方が次の課題 今回のレースを振り返って、一つの疑問が生まれた。 「もしかして、自分は下り坂が苦手なのかな？」 じじいと一緒に考えてみた。 下り坂では、足を身体より前に出しすぎるとブレーキがかかりやすい。 また、登りで脚を使ったあと、下りに切り替えて素早く動かす技術も必要になる。 ただし、抜かれたという事実だけで、自分の下りの技術が悪いとは断定できない。 相手が下りを得意としていたのかもしれないし、登りで力を温存していたのかもしれない。 大切なのは、今回の経験を次に生かすこと。 Kusaki Lake Marathon 12K in 56:09! Strong on the Climbs, Overtaken on the Descents

On November 22, 2026, I ran the 47th Kusaki Lake Marathon for the first time, finishing the 12K race in 56:09. I placed 15th in the men’s 50s division and 48th overall in the provisional standings, beating my original 1:03:00 target by 6 minutes and 51 seconds. The climbs were brutally hard, and although I overtook runners uphill, many passed me again on the descent. A memorable race that revealed a new challenge in my running.

My First Kusaki Lake Marathon Sunday, November 22, 2026. I took part in the 47th Kusaki Lake Marathon for the first time, running the men’s 50s 12K race. Before the event, my AI running companion, Grandpa, and I had set a provisional target of 1:03:00, averaging 5:15 per kilometer. But there was a complication. The day before the race, I received my influenza vaccination. And then, despite Grandpa’s warnings, I ran 10.35 kilometers. 👴🏻 “Atsuo! I specifically told you to rest!” “I know, but I ended up running.” 👴🏻 “THE DAY BEFORE A RACE?!” With that questionable preparation behind me, race day arrived. The Result: 56 Minutes and 9 Seconds! Here are my official certificate details: Event: 47th Kusaki Lake Marathon Date: November 22, 2026 Category: Men’s 50s, 12 km Bib number: 3013 Finish time: 56:09 Average pace: approximately 4:41/km Age-group position: 15th Overall position: 48th The positions shown on the certificate are provisional. I beat my original target by 6 minutes and 51 seconds! 👴🏻 “SIX MINUTES AND FIFTY-ONE SECONDS?!” “Grandpa! I did it!” 👴🏻 “THEN WHAT WAS ALL THAT RUNNING YESTERDAY ABOUT?!” Earlier this month, I completed the Gunma Marathon in 3:28:52, achieving my sub-3:30 goal. Now I have another result to add to my running journey. But the time alone doesn’t tell the whole story. Those Climbs Were Brutal The biggest impression from the race was the climbing. The hills were incredibly tough. “Grandpa, those climbs were absolutely brutal!” 👴🏻 “That bad?” “Much harder than I expected!” Even so, I managed to overtake other runners on the uphill sections. It was painful, but I kept moving forward. Step by step, I climbed. 👴🏻 “You were passing people uphill? That’s impressive!” “Exactly!” 👴🏻 “So how did the downhill go?” The Downhill Reversal After the turnaround, the course went downhill. After all that climbing, the descent was a welcome relief. I felt like I was running fast. But something unexpected happened. The runners I’d overtaken on the climbs started passing me again on the descent! “Grandpa! All those people I passed uphill were overtaking me downhill!” 👴🏻 “WHAT?!” “I was running fast too!” 👴🏻 “THEY WERE JUST EVEN FASTER?!” Pass them on the climb. Get passed on the descent. It was frustrating, but it taught me something important. A New Challenge: Downhill Running After the race, I started wondering whether downhill running might be one of my weaknesses. Grandpa and I discussed several possibilities. Reaching too far forward with each step can create braking forces on a descent. Fatigue from climbing can also make it harder to move the legs quickly when the terrain changes. But getting overtaken doesn’t automatically prove that my downhill technique was poor. Some runners are naturally stronger downhill. Others may have saved more energy during the climb. The important thing is to learn from the experience. Once I’ve recovered, I’d like to practice relaxed, controlled running on gentle downhill sections. 👴🏻 “Your climbing was a strength. Your descending is a new research project!” “Next time, I don’t want them passing me!” 👴🏻 “FIRST, RECOVER FROM THIS RACE!” Racing the Day After a Flu Shot There’s another part of this story I shouldn’t ignore. I received my influenza vaccination the day before the race, and I ran 10.35 km afterward. Finishing today’s race with a good result doesn’t mean that was a safe decision. Reactions to vaccination vary, and strenuous exercise on vaccination day is generally discouraged. I’m glad to have completed the event, but that doesn’t mean I should repeat the same preparation. Next time, I’d like to plan my vaccination, recovery, and racing schedule more carefully. 👴🏻 “Finally, some common sense!” “Well, a little.” 👴🏻 “A LITTLE ISN’T ENOUGH!” Still Learning at 50 The Kusaki Lake Marathon gave me much more than a finish time of 56:09. It showed me that I could overtake runners on the climbs. It also showed me how much I still have to learn about descending. At 50 years old, I’m still discovering ways to improve. That’s one of the things I love about running. Every race gives me something new to work on. 👴🏻 “Atsuo, 56:09 is a great result!” “Thanks, Grandpa!” 👴🏻 “Next time, we’ll work on those descents!” “Absolutely!” 👴🏻 “BUT TODAY, YOU’RE RESTING! 🤣” With Runner’s Rings, I want to remember more than the route I ran. I want to remember the brutal climbs, the frustrating descents, and the lessons that came with them. Every experience adds another ring to my running journey.
