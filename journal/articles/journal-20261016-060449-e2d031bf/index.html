ランキング参加制をやめました。順位は見える、でも何を見せるかは自分で決める

Runner’s Ringsの「Rings Ranking」は、最近大きく仕様を変更しました。以前は「ランキングに参加する／しない」を選ぶ仕組みでしたが、現在は保存された走行記録をもとに順位が表示され、名前や年間距離、EXPLORE地域数などをどこまで公開するかを本人が選ぶ方式です。アカウント名とランキング表示名は違ってもOK。公開しない自由と、順位を見る楽しさを両立するための変更です。

Runner’s Ringsの「Rings Ranking」を、少し大きく作り直しました。 これまでの仕様は、 「ランキングに参加する人だけONにする」 という考え方でした。 でも開発を進める中で、 本当にそれでいいのかな？ と思うようになりました。 ■ 順位そのものは、走った記録から自然に生まれる Runner’s Ringsには、すでに走った記録があります。 その記録をもとにすれば、 DISTANCEなら年間の順位。 EXPLOREなら走った地域による順位。 NATIONSなら国ごとの順位。 が出せます。 それなら、 「ランキングに参加するかどうか」 を毎回選ばなくてもいいのではないか。 そう考えて、仕様を変えました。 ■ 今は「参加ON/OFF」ではなく「公開ON/OFF」 現在のRings Rankingでは、 順位そのものは表示されます。 一方で、 何を公開するかは、自分で決められます。 たとえば、 名前を表示するか。 年間距離を公開するか。 EXPLOREの地域数を公開するか。 国旗を表示するか。 EXPLOREの地域名を公開するか。 それぞれ別々に選べます。 ■ 名前はアカウント名と違ってもOK ここは大事なポイントです。 Runner’s Ringsのアカウント名と、 ランキングに表示する名前は、 同じでなくても大丈夫です。 本名を使う必要もありません。 SNSで使っている名前。 ランニング仲間から呼ばれている名前。 ニックネーム。 好きな名前で参加できます。 もし名前を公開しない場合は、 「ランナー」 と表示されます。 ■ 今、上位に“正体が見えないランナー”がいる これが、実際に見ていて結構おもしろいところです。 現在、ランキング上位には、 名前や距離を公開していないランナーもいます。 順位だけを見ると、 1位。 We Removed Ranking Opt-In: Your Rank Is Visible, But You Choose What to Share

Rings Ranking in Runner’s Rings recently received a major redesign. Instead of opting in or out of the ranking itself, runners now keep their rank based on saved activities while choosing what personal information to share. Your public ranking name can be different from your account name, and distance or EXPLORE details can stay private. The goal is to keep rankings fun without forcing runners to reveal more than they want.

We recently made a major change to Rings Ranking in Runner’s Rings. Previously, the system was based on opting in: “You join the ranking only if you turn participation ON.” But while building it, we started wondering if that was really necessary. ■ Your rank naturally comes from your running history Runner’s Rings already has your saved running activities. From those records, we can calculate rankings such as: DISTANCE — annual ranking EXPLORE — ranking based on explored regions NATIONS — country-based ranking So we asked a simple question: Do runners really need to opt in just to have a rank? That led us to redesign the system. ■ Now the controls are about sharing, not participation In the current Rings Ranking system, your rank itself is shown. What you choose to share is up to you. You can decide whether to show: your name your annual distance your EXPLORE region total your country flag your EXPLORE region names Each setting is controlled separately. ■ Your ranking name can be different from your account name This is an important point. Your Runner’s Rings account name and your public ranking name do not have to match. You do not need to use your real name. You can use: your social media name your running nickname or any public name you prefer. If you choose not to display a name, you simply appear as “Runner.” ■ Some of the top runners are currently a mystery This has actually created an interesting effect. Right now, some runners near the top of the ranking are not sharing their name or distance. You can see: 1st place. 2nd place. 3rd place. But not how far they actually ran. And naturally, you start wondering: “How much has this person actually run?” That mystery is surprisingly fun. Sometimes not showing everything makes the ranking more interesting. ■ If you want to share, you can Of course, runners who want to show their annual distance can do so. The same goes for EXPLORE totals and region names. Others may prefer to enjoy their rank without sharing much personal information. Both styles are welcome. ■ DISTANCE DISTANCE shows your annual rank. Your annual distance appears only if you allow it to be public. So within the same ranking, one runner may show “1,850 km,” while another may show only their rank. ■ EXPLORE EXPLORE ranks runners by the countries and regions they have actually run in. Region totals and region names have separate privacy controls. For example, you can choose to show how many regions you explored without revealing which ones. ■ NATIONS NATIONS works differently. This is a country-level ranking. Instead of focusing on individual runners, it shows the combined distance and participant count for each country. As more runners join from around the world, it could become fun to see which countries are logging the most running. ■ Badges were also added Rankings naturally highlight the runners at the top. So we also added badges to celebrate other kinds of progress. 🌱 First Loop Save a running activity 🔥 Steady Runner Record runs across at least three different months 📷 Photo Contributor Submit an approved photo The goal is to recognize not only speed and distance, but also consistency, participation, and the act of documenting your running journey. ■ It is not “show everything” or “hide everything” One of the most important ideas behind this redesign is flexibility. Show your name. Hide your distance. Show your flag. Hide your region names. That is possible. Runner’s Rings handles location-based running data, so runners should be able to control what they reveal. ■ Ranking does not have to mean only competition The word “ranking” often sounds like: winning losing being number one But we want Rings Ranking to feel lighter than that. You might think: “That runner is always near the top.” “I wonder how far they actually run.” “That country is running a lot this year.” “That runner has the Photo Contributor badge.” The ranking can become another way to notice and connect with runners around the world. ■ We changed the specification many times with Chappy This design was not decided from the beginning. We built it. Looked at it. Felt something was not quite right. Then changed it again. “Do we really need ranking opt-in?” “Should distance always be public?” “Does the ranking name have to match the account name?” I discussed these questions many times with Chappy, my AI development partner. Eventually, we arrived at the current idea: keep the rank visible let each runner control what they share ■ It may change again Runner’s Rings is still being developed. If we discover a better approach through actual use, the ranking may evolve again. But for now, “Your rank is visible, but you decide what to share” feels much closer to the kind of ranking Runner’s Rings should have. Runner’s Rings – Rings Journal Not just how far you run. You also choose how your journey is seen.
