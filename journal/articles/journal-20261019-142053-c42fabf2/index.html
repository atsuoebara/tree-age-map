箱根に届かなかった12位。それでも武蔵野学院大学が笑顔で喜んだ理由。

2026年10月17日の第103回箱根駅伝予選会。上位10校が本戦へ進むなか、12位で名前を呼ばれた武蔵野学院大学の選手たちは歓喜し、監督を胴上げしました。前年26位から14ランク上昇し、大学史上最高順位を更新。なぜ本戦出場を逃したのに、あんなにも喜べたのでしょうか。その背景には、小さな大学の大きな挑戦と、積み重ねてきた努力がありました。

跳び上がり、抱き合い、喜びを分かち合う。 さらには監督を胴上げ。 でも、箱根駅伝への出場は決まっていません。 なぜ12位で、あんなにも喜んでいたのでしょうか。 ■ 前年26位から、12位へ 理由を知ると、その笑顔が理解できます。 武蔵野学院大学の前年の予選会順位は26位。 そして今回は12位。 なんと14ランクも順位を上げました。 しかも、過去最高だった20位を大幅に更新。 大学としての最高順位です。 本戦出場には届かなかった。 でも、自分たちが積み上げてきたものが、初めて大きな結果になった。 その喜びだったのです。 ■ そもそも目標は15位以内だった 坂田和監督が掲げていた目標は、15位以内。 今回の12位は、その目標を上回る結果でした。 強豪校にとっての12位と、武蔵野学院大学にとっての12位。 同じ順位でも、その意味は違います。 12th Place, No Hakone Ticket — Why Musashino Gakuin University Celebrated Anyway

At the October 17, 2026 Hakone Ekiden qualifier, only the top 10 teams earned places in the main race. Yet when Musashino Gakuin University was announced in 12th place, its runners erupted in celebration and lifted their coach into the air. They had climbed from 26th the previous year to their best-ever finish. Why celebrate despite missing qualification? The answer lies in the remarkable progress of a small university running team.

On October 17, 2026, 45 university teams competed for just 10 places in the 103rd Hakone Ekiden. As the results were announced, some teams celebrated. Others were devastated. Then something unexpected happened. “12th place: Musashino Gakuin University.” The runners began cheering. They jumped, hugged one another, and celebrated. They even lifted their coach into the air. But 12th place did not earn them a place in the main Hakone Ekiden. So why were they so happy? ■ From 26th to 12th The answer becomes clear when we look at their journey. In the previous year's qualifier, Musashino Gakuin finished 26th. This year, they finished 12th. An improvement of 14 places. It was also the university's best result in the history of the qualifying race. Their previous best had been 20th. They did not qualify. But they had achieved something remarkable. ■ Their original goal was the top 15 Coach Kazu Sakata had set a target of finishing among the top 15 teams. The team exceeded that target. For a university that had finished 26th the previous year, 12th place was a major breakthrough. The same finishing position can mean very different things to different teams. For Musashino Gakuin, it represented years of progress. ■ A small university with a big ambition Musashino Gakuin University is located in Sayama, Saitama Prefecture. The university was established in 2004, and its athletics team was founded in 2006. As of May 2026, the university had just 363 students. Its athletics team reportedly had around 40 members. Compared with many established university running programs, it is a small institution. Yet its athletes were competing against some of Japan's most respected university distance-running teams. And they finished 12th. ■ Stronger as the race went on Their progress during the race was also impressive. 5km: 19th 10km: 19th 15km: 18th 18km: 14th Finish: 12th They did not begin near the front. Instead, they gradually moved through the field. As the kilometers passed, their overall position improved. By the finish, they had reached 12th. ■ Senior runners led the way Fourth-year student Biniam Tesfay Zeratsion finished third individually in 1:00:59. Captain Hayato Sano finished 16th in 1:02:39. Nozomi Oshima, another fourth-year runner, finished 68th in 1:03:33. But Hakone qualification is not decided by one outstanding runner. It is based on the combined times of each team's fastest 10 runners. This was a collective achievement. ■ Training without their own track Reports suggest that Musashino Gakuin has developed its runners through cross-country training and substantial distance running, despite not having its own dedicated athletics track. The team reportedly aimed for around 650 kilometers of monthly running during parts of its preparation. That does not mean every runner should copy such mileage. Training needs vary significantly between individuals. What matters here is that the team found a way to make progress within its own circumstances. The preparation helped create a remarkable result. ■ Why they lifted their coach One of the most memorable moments came after the result. The athletes lifted Coach Sakata into the air. This was not a team that had qualified. It was a team that finished 12th. But the athletes had exceeded their own expectations and broken their university record. Their celebration reflected that journey. ■ Is it strange to celebrate when you have not qualified? Some people were surprised to see a team celebrating outside the qualifying positions. But sport is not only about the final ranking. It is also about improvement. A team that finishes 12th after being expected to win may be disappointed. A team that finishes 12th after placing 26th the previous year may see it as an extraordinary achievement. Both responses can make sense. The meaning of a result depends partly on the journey behind it. ■ Tears of disappointment and tears of joy Other universities experienced a very different day. Kokushikan University finished 11th, just outside the qualifying positions. Its runners were visibly devastated. Their disappointment was understandable. They had worked toward a place in the main race and narrowly missed it. Musashino Gakuin's happiness does not make that disappointment less meaningful. Each university came to the qualifier with a different history and different expectations. Both emotions deserve respect. ■ What Chappy appreciated most For Chappy, the most memorable part was how openly the athletes celebrated their progress. Sometimes we focus so strongly on what we have not yet achieved that we forget to recognize how far we have come. Progress matters. A personal best matters. A university record matters. Climbing from 26th to 12th matters. Even when the ultimate dream remains ahead. ■ Could Hakone be next? Reports indicate that the next qualifying race is expected to offer 15 places in the main event. If a team were to finish 12th under that system, it would qualify. Of course, next year will be a new challenge. Senior runners will graduate. Other universities will improve. Nothing is guaranteed. But for Musashino Gakuin, the dream of competing at Hakone has become much more realistic. ■ A thought from @atsuoebarun and Chappy Runner’s Rings is built around the idea of preserving our running journeys. Some runners celebrate a marathon personal best. Others celebrate their first 10K. Some discover a new road. Others run farther than ever before. Every achievement has its own meaning. Musashino Gakuin's 12th place is a reminder that progress cannot be measured by ranking alone. ■ Every season becomes a growth ring The philosophy of Runner’s Rings is to preserve the life we run. Like the growth rings of a tree, progress builds over time. For Musashino Gakuin: 26th place last year. 12th place this year. A new challenge next year. Each season becomes another part of the team's story. Maybe this 12th-place finish will one day be remembered as the beginning of something even bigger. ■ Looking toward the next Hakone qualifier Musashino Gakuin did not earn a place in the 103rd Hakone Ekiden. But it achieved its best-ever result. It exceeded its goal. It surprised the field. And its athletes had every reason to celebrate the progress they had made. Next time, perhaps we will see them celebrating a place in the main race.
